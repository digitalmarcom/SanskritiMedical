MBBS Program Overview

The MBBS (Bachelor of Medicine and Bachelor of Surgery) is the primary undergraduate medical degree offered at Sanskriti Santosh Medical College & Research Centre. The program is approved by the National Medical Commission (NMC), Government of India, and is conducted under the UPUMS affiliation.

Our MBBS program follows the Competency-Based Medical Education (CBME) framework introduced by the NMC — a transformative curriculum that emphasizes practical skills, clinical reasoning, professional values, and lifelong learning alongside traditional academic knowledge.

Program Highlights

  • NMC-approved CBME curriculum (2019)
  • 5.5-year program (4.5 academic + 1-year internship)
  • 464-bed teaching hospital for clinical training
  • High-fidelity simulation labs
  • Mandatory foundation course and early clinical exposure

Curriculum — Competency-Based Medical Education

SSMCRC implements the NMC's 2019 CBME curriculum which replaces rote memorization with a patient-centered, outcome-driven approach. The curriculum integrates basic sciences with clinical sciences from the first year onwards.

Foundation Course (1 Month)

Mandatory induction covering: Communication skills, Professionalism, Research basics, Basic Life Support, Computer literacy, and campus integration.

Early Clinical Exposure (ECE)

Integrated clinical visits from Phase 1 itself — correlating basic science topics with real patient scenarios at Sanskriti Chikitsalaya.

Elective Postings

4-week student elective in Phase 3 Part 2 — allowing students to explore specialties of interest including community postings, research, or clinical specialties.

Attitude, Ethics & Communication (AETCOM)

A longitudinal AETCOM module integrated across all 4.5 years covering medical ethics, professional attitudes, doctor-patient communication, and humanities.

Teaching Methodology

Lecture-Based Learning

Traditional didactic lectures in smart, AV-equipped lecture halls with interactive elements, Q&A, and recorded content for revision.

Small Group Learning

Problem-Based Learning (PBL), case-based discussions (CBD), and team-based learning (TBL) in tutorial groups of 8–12 students.

Simulation Training

High-fidelity patient simulators, task trainers, and standardized patient exercises for clinical skill development in a safe environment.

Bedside Teaching

Daily ward rounds, case presentations, and clinical demonstrations at Sanskriti Chikitsalaya — supervised by experienced consultants.

Lab-Based Learning

Hands-on anatomy dissections, physiology practicals, pathology slide reading, microbiology culture work, and pharmacology experiments.

Community Postings

Structured rural and urban community health postings as part of Community Medicine training — PHCs, sub-centres, and health camps.

Assessment System

SSMCRC follows the UPUMS examination system with Internal Assessment (IA) and University (Professional) Examinations as prescribed by NMC.

Formative Assessment (Internal)

  • Theory tests (periodically)
  • Practical and viva assessments
  • DOAP (Directly Observed Assessment of Procedural skills)
  • Log Book / Portfolio maintenance
  • OSPE / OSCE examinations

Summative (University) Examinations

  • Theory papers (Multiple Choice + Long / Short essays)
  • Practical examinations
  • Clinical and viva voce
  • Conducted by UPUMS as per scheduled dates
  • Internal assessment weightage included
A minimum of 75% attendance in theory and 80% in practicals is mandatory to appear in UPUMS university examinations.

Medical Education Unit (MEU)

The Medical Education Unit at SSMCRC is dedicated to academic quality, faculty development, curriculum innovation, and student assessment reform. The MEU serves as the engine of continuous improvement in medical education at the institution.

Faculty Development Programs

Regular workshops on CBME implementation, assessment techniques, and teaching methodologies for all faculty members.

Curriculum Mapping

Systematic mapping of competencies to learning objectives, teaching methods, and assessment tools across all phases.

Assessment Reform

Formative assessment innovations including OSPE, OSCE, Direct Observation tools, and portfolio-based assessment.

Attendance Rules

Theory Attendance

Minimum 75% attendance in theory classes is mandatory to be eligible for UPUMS university examinations.

Practical Attendance

Minimum 80% attendance in practical sessions is mandatory as per NMC and UPUMS regulations.

Clinical Postings

100% completion of mandatory clinical posting hours as prescribed in the CBME curriculum for each department.

Condonation

Medical grounds condonation up to 10% may be considered on application with valid medical certificate, subject to institutional and UPUMS rules.

Students who fail to maintain minimum attendance will not be eligible to appear in UPUMS examinations and will be required to repeat the academic year.
